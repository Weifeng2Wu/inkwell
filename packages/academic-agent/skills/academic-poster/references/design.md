# Poster composition

Use the paper's evidence to choose the hierarchy. A short title, clear central finding and one strong result figure should be readable before the detailed method. Keep claims bounded by the reported results, and include meaningful limitations rather than promotional conclusions.

For A0, start with a 20–28 mm page margin, 15–20 mm column gaps, 18–24 mm title type, 10–13 mm section headings and 7–9 mm body type. Scale for smaller sheets. Keep body copy concise (often 350–650 words across an A0 poster); figure labels must remain readable at final size. Avoid shrinking text to fit more content.

Classic works well with two columns in portrait and three in landscape. Minimal can use a large takeaway followed by a wide figure and short supporting columns. Modern can center a prominent finding with supporting evidence and a small method strip. All three should use clear alignment, consistent spacing, ample whitespace and an unambiguous reading order. The style field controls palette and font; element geometry encodes the composition.

Charts, diagrams and images need captions explaining the comparison and relevant units, sample sizes or uncertainty when present in the paper. Use labels as well as color to distinguish conditions. Do not invent numerical chart data by visually estimating a plot. When only the original figure is available, embed it and preserve attribution. Add a concise reference list and acknowledgements only when supported by the source.

Before delivery, check every figure is resolvable, all boxes fit the page, the title and authors are accurate, every quantitative claim matches the paper, text has sufficient space, and the objects remain independently editable. Keep a compact note of missing material outside the poster rather than printing implementation instructions on it.
